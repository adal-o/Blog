<h1>“Tip the world on its side and everything loose will land in Los Angeles”</h1>

<h1>- Frank Lloyd Wright</h1>

<img src="{{ site.baseUrl }}static/images/IMG_6129.heic" style="width: 100%; aspect-ratio: auto">

<p>My name is Raine and I am a girl from the valley. For the past seven months, I have been living in Japan; in a town in Saitama on the outskirts of Tokyo. It seems I am always situated on the brink of a larger, more noteworthy location and perhaps that is the most defining thing about me.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>Identity has been on the noggin lately, especially since I have had the two halves of my heart stretched across the pacific ocean. I am often thinking about how I define myself, especially as I live in a country that is very homogenous and I am definitely not a part of that homogeneity. (perhaps a conversation for a different time)</p>

<p>Am I a collection of sex, gender, sexuality, ethnic background, race, religion, and economic status?</p>

<p>I think such identity markers are integral but limiting and the genesis of increasingly tired discourse and generalizations (with love I point to my mixed brothers and sisters and the buzzfeed style “you know you’re [...] if […]” content).</p>

<p>I’d like to think myself more multitudinous than my categorization on a government document.</p>

<p>Perhaps I would be better defined by one of the many personality metrics: mbti, enneagram, blood type or horoscope. But most people guess my mbti right away and never guess my sign so this feels hit or miss.</p>

<p>While I think these things all intertwine into the complicated mosaic of what defines a person, I feel far more defined by the teetering. I often find myself with both feet on a line: between locations, between passions, between unbridled joy and unending fraughtness. I suppose it could be characterized as a perpetual indecision but I feel quite content and grateful in my proclivity for balancing.</p>

<p>I think identity is ineffable, that a person contains so much within them that it is impossible to categorize them neatly into a little package and assume things about them as a result. But I also think it’s very human that we desire such heuristics, I am also at fault for this. In fact, it’s probably the impossibility of it that makes it such an intoxicating task.</p>

<p>And yet, I have chosen to introduce myself as such:</p>

<p>My name is Raine and I am a girl from the valley.</p>

<p>This is perhaps the most all encompassing and multitudinous way I know how to describe myself: in my relation to the city of Los Angeles, its ineffable magic, and the slightly contested validity of the Valley in Los Angeles (i will tell you now that the Valley has been and always will be LA). I am LA; not just in that it is the city where both my parents moved to or where I was born or where I went to school and met so many of my friends, but also in that it too teeters. LA is a city of isolation yet interconnectedness, of encompassing and exclusion, of dreams and disappointment, of contrast, of contradiction, of light and darkness, beauty and pain, of trying and success and failure.</p>

<p>The theme of Angel Outsider is “dedicated to Los Angeles.” This is written with the love of LA in our hearts but also because we are a product of LA in that it contains everything we are. And while what we write will weave in and out of the topic of LA, the theme will always remain. It’s all LA. I am all LA.</p>

<p>I am a girl from the valley and this is my hello.</p>
