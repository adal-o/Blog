In From the Valley and Out to LA

6/29/2026
By Amelie Wu

My name is Amelie, and like Raine, I too am a girl from the Valley.

Although my identity is not solely defined by place, the Valley remains very dearly at the core of my person and I gladly claim it as home. I never once doubted that it was considered a part of Los Angeles until I eventually encountered opposition from those who were raised on the other side of the Santa Monica Mountains. I began to realize that the Valley was often punctuated by contestation, situated on the precipice of beyond and too removed to be so easily welcomed (I imagined the teasing idea of a dangling carrot when laying out this framework).

It is this idea of temptation that seems to have informed the way I view myself in relation to this positionality. As a girl from the Valley, I tread between the allure of something larger than me and a frustratingly stifled but steadfast internal self – potential persisting to become against the push and pull of internal and external expression.

With this in mind, I offer a twofold introduction.

The Valley

I describe myself as reserved, not shy. I am careful to make this distinction having actually been a once-shy person up until high school. As I began to consider identity and nurture a more conscious sense of self, I grew earnest to move past this label.

Still, I see more than what I say and I calculate before I express. Even as I emerged from the markers of shyness, its foundation remained engrained in the way I operate. I remain more observant than I’d sometimes like to be and continue to struggle with outwardly expressing myself. I still bite my tongue and harbor thoughts when I miss the window to say it out loud. I am troubled by perception, and on a more heightened note, misperception. As a result, I often pinpoint myself as my own biggest limitation.

What I didn’t realize at the time, was that the seeds of shyness had conceived my internal self. Observation advised intuition and calculation helped me place my energy in the right places. I have since come to believe that this inward expression was both molded and held by the Valley, and of course, the deep gratitude I now have for a formerly shy Amelie.

In this regard, the Valley has embraced me in its sanctuary throughout my childhood, adolescence, and current 20-somethings. The mountains enclosing its grounds created the garden bed that nourished my foundation of self that grew evermore enticed to step into the beyond.

The Broader Los Angeles

A friend once told me that I am simultaneously a dreamer and a realist. I had never heard anything so accurate and her statement compelled me to assess its application.

As a dreamer, I am always searching for something more. I grew up traversing the city to satisfy desire. A short drive along the 101 could take me to craveable meals in Thai Town and a further drive down the 210 became a frequent ritual when my family sought Chinese comfort food and groceries. I am creative and I often enjoy ideating above executing (though I challenge myself to nurture a balance of both). I feel deeply and allow emotions to guide me.

As a realist, I only allow emotions to guide me to a certain extent and I create within the confines of what I believe myself to be capable of. I often downplay what I truly want and place a cap on my ambitions. Here is where the bounds of the Valley frequently present themselves.

Despite self-imposed limitations and the lasting faults of my shyness, the broader Los Angeles transfixed me with its own vastness and neverending breadth of possibility. While the Valley offered me a ground to establish my footing, the broader Los Angeles challenged me to push my own sensibilities, stretching discovery and exploration. It became more and more clear that the two went hand in hand and that the duality of my identity was in fact indivisible.

✦

Though I posited my introduction as dichotomous, I seek to bridge the two parts with the notion that one cannot exist without the other. The Valley shapes Los Angeles just as much as Los Angeles shapes the Valley. In echo, my former shyness and hindrance informs my very dreaming, my judgement, and my becoming.

All this to say, I stand firm in my belief that the Valley is a part of Los Angeles. And Los Angeles, sprawling as it is interconnected, is inevitably all-encompassing, everywhere and nowhere, and belonging to all.
